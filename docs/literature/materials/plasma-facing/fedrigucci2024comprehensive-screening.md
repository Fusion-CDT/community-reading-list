A systematic search for alternatives to tungsten as a plasma-facing material for the divertor. Despite being the material of choice for ITER, tungsten has well-known problems: cracking and embrittlement, surface damage from helium (bubbles and "fuzz"), and poor resistance to oxidation if air gets in.

The authors "screen" (i.e. systematically filter) known inorganic materials in two stages. First, they rank candidates from a materials database by whether they can withstand divertor heat loads in an ITER-like tokamak. Then they use density functional theory to assess two key weaknesses of the survivors: sputtering of surface atoms, and how readily hydrogen is taken up into the lattice.

The screening recovers the materials already known to work well (tungsten, molybdenum and carbon-based materials), which is a good sign that it's reliable, and also points to some less familiar refractory materials worth studying further.

A good overview of what makes a plasma-facing material work well, and of why tungsten is so hard to replace.
