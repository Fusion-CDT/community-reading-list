A review of the facilities used to test plasma-facing materials and components before they are used in a fusion device. No single facility can reproduce the conditions inside a tokamak, so testing relies on a chain of devices, each capturing part of the picture:

- **High heat flux test stands** (electron and ion beams, e.g. JUDITH and GLADIS) apply steady and ELM-like heat loads to anything from small samples to full-size components.
- **Linear plasma devices** (e.g. PISCES, Magnum-PSI and PSI-2) expose samples to a well-controlled plasma, to study erosion and hydrogen isotope retention under first wall and divertor conditions.
- **Neutron irradiation** studies, and testing of irradiated samples in hot cells.
- **Exposure in tokamaks and stellarators**, using manipulators that insert test pieces without opening the vessel.

A useful map of who can test what, and where. Read this if you want to understand where experimental results on plasma-facing materials come from, or if you're choosing a facility for your own experiments. The reference list is a good starting point for the literature on each type of test.
