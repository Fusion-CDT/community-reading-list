A review of how damage and microstructure in tungsten change how much hydrogen and helium it traps. This matters because hydrogen isotopes trapped in the wall add to the tritium inventory, which is limited for safety reasons, and because helium forms bubbles and "fuzz" that degrade the surface.

The review covers experimental and modelling work on the different features that affect retention: pre-existing damage (e.g. from irradiation), simultaneous bombardment by several ion species, impurity and alloying elements, defects such as dislocations and grain boundaries, and nanostructured tungsten.

Good for anyone studying the microstructure of tungsten, especially the effects of hydrogen and helium.
